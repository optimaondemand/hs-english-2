2. Module 1 Major-benchmark coverage at a glance

Module 1 is an epic/narrative-craft module, so it carries the reading benchmarks that fit narrative literary analysis and classical-text study heavily, and it launches but does not complete the writing/oral benchmarks that recur across all four modules. It does not touch the informational-speech benchmarks (R.2.2/R.2.3/R.2.4), which are reserved for the Q4 American-speeches unit — flagged explicitly in §5 below so no one assumes they are covered here.

Benchmark Covered in M1? Primary carrier

4. Project and recitation — consolidated mapping

The Bard's Hall (module project, all 9 weekly Quick Writes as feeders + final): - ELA.10.C.1.2 — primary (narrative pacing/tension/mood/tone is the entire point of the project) - ELA.10.C.1.5 — Wk 8→9 revision cycle - ELA.10.C.3.1 — conventions folded into every feeder and the final rubric - ELA.10.V.1.1 — Word Study words used accurately across Quick Writes - ELA.10.R.3.3 — the project is explicitly an "Odyssey-style" adaptation, so it doubles as evidence of understanding classical-text adaptation

Recitation (Q1, double-duty with the anchor text): - ELA.10.C.2.1 — primary (oral presentation, clear perspective, volume/pronunciation/pacing per the CPALMS clarification) - ELA.10.R.3.3 — the recited passage (proem or Book 9 self-naming) is itself the classical text - ELA.10.EE.6.1 — voice/tone, supporting

5. Gaps — NOT covered in Module 1 (flagged per _BUILD-CONVENTIONS.md §14)

  • R.2.2, R.2.3, R.2.4 (historical American speeches and essays — central idea, author's purpose/appeals, comparing opposing arguments) require a Q4 American-speeches text set. Not covered anywhere in Module 1. Do not claim these are met until the Q4 module is built.
  • R.2.1 (informational text structures/features) is likewise reserved for a Q4 research-heavy module; Module 1 is narrative-only and does not exercise informational text structures.
  • R.1.4 (layers of meaning/ambiguity in a poem) is only lightly defensible in M1: the Butler translation used for on-page quotes is prose, not verse, so if this benchmark needs a true poem, it is better carried in Q3 (A Midsummer Night's Dream, verse) or Q4 poetry supplements. [VERIFY] whether the recitation passage (proem) should count as "a poem" for this benchmark, given it is quoted here as translated prose — flag for the M1 pilot review rather than claim coverage.
  • C.1.3 (argument), C.1.4 (expository), C.4.1 (research), C.5.1/C.5.2 (digital presentation/collaborative platforms) are not this module's writing mode. Expect these to be carried by the Q2 Oedipus "Trial" module (argument/research) and later modules.
  • R.3.4 (rhetoric) gets only light, incidental treatment in M1 (Odysseus's persuasive storytelling) — do not report this as a full R.3.4 unit; save the fuller rhetorical-appeals treatment for Q2 Oedipus, which is more argument-centered.

5b. Added 2026-08-01 (Mississippi diocesan crosswalk; see _BUILD-CONVENTIONS.md §14b)

Source of record: _DIOCESAN-CROSSWALK-English2.md, built from the Diocese of Jackson English II standards (printed pp. 100-107 of Master ELA 2024-2025.pdf, read in full) against the same CPALMS 1001340 export used for §1 above.

Changed in Module 1: - M1_W7_ProjectPitch_Discussion.md revised. Added a Step 0 in which the class collectively sets the year's discussion ground rules and a reply-timing norm, and split the two required replies into distinct roles: Challenger (specific feedback plus a real question) and Synthesizer (name where the thread agrees, where it splits, and what did or did not move in your own thinking). Workload unchanged: still one post and two replies, with Step 0 absorbed into the existing post score, so §12a weights are untouched. - Closes diocesan Speaking and Listening, Comprehension and Collaboration 1.2 (ground rules, decision-making, individual roles) and 1.4 (summarizing points of agreement and disagreement, qualifying or justifying one's own view), neither of which was met anywhere before. 1.1 and 1.3 were already met by the pitch and the feedback replies. - Consequences for §2 above: EE.4.1 was rated Light for M1. With this revision it is genuinely covered for the discussion strand, though M1 remains a solo-async module overall. C.1.5 is also strengthened, since the Synthesizer reply requires a student to state what they will do with feedback rather than only receive it. - TX TEKS §110.37(c)(1)(D): §5a above recorded this as covering "the collaborative-feedback portion only, not the consensus-building/decision-making clause." That caveat is now partly discharged. Step 0 is literally setting ground rules for decision making, so 1(D) is met substantially here. The fullest decision-making case is the optional M2_W9_Council_Deliberation_MS_Discussion.md.

Diocesan standards Module 1 already met without changes, now credited explicitly (they were invisible in this map because no FL benchmark names them): - Writing, Range of Writing 1 (write routinely over extended and shorter time frames): met by the nine weekly Quick Writes plus the nine-week sustained Bard's Hall project. Florida has no volume-of-writing benchmark. - Reading Literature, Craft and Structure 2 (how an author structures a text, orders events, and manipulates time to create tension or surprise): substantively met by L1.03 Starting in the Middle (in medias res), L7.03 What the Reader Knows (dramatic irony), and L8.03 Pacing and Suspense. FL's R.2.1 covers text structure for informational text only, so this had no FL host. The terms flashback and parallel plot are still not used anywhere in M1; adding them to L1.03 would make coverage explicit rather than inferred. Low-cost improvement, not yet done. - Reading Literature, Craft and Structure 3 (point of view or cultural experience in literature from outside the United States, drawing on wide reading of world literature): substantively met, since the anchor is Homer and M1 teaches Greek cultural context heavily. But the phrase "world literature" appears nowhere in the course, and the standard's comparative framing is never named. Flagged, not closed. The crosswalk recommends closing it in the Q4 capstone synthesis, comparing the Greek, Early Modern English, and American works. Do not claim it as met from M1 alone.

Still not covered in Module 1 (see the M2 map §5b for where these land): fallacy identification, speaker evaluation, cross-medium comparison, and accurate summary as a taught skill.
