Week 9 · Discussion brief
The Council Deliberates
Read this page, then post in the Canvas discussion
📖 WHAT THIS WEEK ASKS OF YOU
You have argued your case. Now you have to sit with the people who argued the other one.
The Council of Thebes does not exist to declare a winner. It exists to reach a verdict the city can live with, and then to say what that verdict means for the city going forward. That is a harder task than winning an argument, and it is the task in front of you now.
🔒 READ THIS FIRST
Your brief is already submitted and graded on its own merits. Nothing you say here can change that score. You are free to say you have reconsidered, and equally free to say that hearing the other side has left you exactly where you were. Your mark here depends only on the quality of your own contribution, never on what the Council concludes or on whether you end up on the winning side.
📚 WHAT TO POST
STEP 1 · OPEN YOUR POSITION FOR REVISION (BY MID-WEEK)
In three or four sentences:
1. State the verdict you argued, and the standard of judgment you argued it by.
2. Name the single strongest thing you heard from the other side, at the tribunal or in the briefs you read. Not the weakest. The strongest.
3. Say what it would take to change your verdict. If the honest answer is that nothing could, say that, and say why. An unfalsifiable position is a real position, but the Council is entitled to know it is dealing with one.
STEP 2 · TAKE A ROLE AND DO ITS JOB (BY END OF WEEK)
Look at the pinned role list in the discussion. Claim the role with the fewest people in it and say in your first line which role you are taking. The four roles and their jobs are below.
STEP 3 · REACH A VERDICT, OR SAY HONESTLY THAT YOU CANNOT (BY END OF WEEK)
Reply once to the Drafter’s proposed verdict, or to the strongest amendment of it. Either endorse it, saying what convinced you, or dissent from it, saying what the Council has failed to account for. A recorded dissent is a complete and honorable outcome. A Council that splits and says so has done its job better than one that pretends to agree.
📚 THE FOUR ROLES

Every role has a different job, and a Council needs all four. Claim the emptiest one, not your favorite. A Council in which everyone wants to be the Drafter decides nothing.

THE RECONCILER
Find two posts that appear to disagree and identify what they actually agree on underneath. Very often the disagreement is about the standard, not the facts. Name it precisely.
THE HOLDOUT
Argue for a verdict that is losing in this thread. You do not have to believe it. Somebody has to make sure the Council’s verdict has been tested rather than assumed.
THE CLERK
Track where the Council actually stands: how many for each verdict, on what standard, and what the one unresolved question is. State it plainly, without arguing for a side.
THE DRAFTER
Propose the Council’s verdict in two sentences: the finding, and what Thebes should do next. Others will amend it, and that is the point.
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
Before you look at which roles are still open, which one are you instinctively drawn to? (No wrong answer.)
📚 HOW TO DO THIS WELL
▶ Consensus is not the goal. An honest, well-mapped disagreement is a better outcome than a false agreement.
▶ You may change your mind. Saying so out loud, after arguing the other way in a graded brief, takes more nerve than holding the line, and it is the single most respectable move available in this thread.
▶ You may also refuse to change your mind. Then your job is to say what would change it.
▶ Argue about the standard of judgment, not about each other. Almost every real disagreement in this case is a disagreement about what guilt requires, not about what Oedipus did.
▶ Bring your listening sheet if you built one. The four columns are exactly what a Council member needs while reading a thread this long.
📝 HOW THIS IS SCORED
LEVEL STEP 1 STEPS 2 AND 3
Full States verdict and standard, names the genuinely strongest opposing point, and answers what would change your mind (including an honest “nothing, because…”) Claims a role and does that role’s job rather than restating your brief, then either endorses or dissents from the proposed verdict with a stated reason
Partial Restates the brief without naming a real opposing strength, or dodges the what-would-change-your-mind question Claims a role but argues your own case under its name, or never replies to the proposed verdict
Missing No post No participation in the deliberation
💪 BEFORE YOU POST

A whole quarter has been spent learning to build an argument and to test one. This is the week you find out what the harder skill is: sitting with people who reached a different verdict from the same evidence, representing their position fairly, and then deciding something anyway. That is what a community does when it disagrees and still has to act.
