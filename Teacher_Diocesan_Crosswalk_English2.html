Purpose: identify every Diocese of Jackson English II standard that Florida's CPALMS English 2 benchmarks do not cover, then say whether the built course already covers it anyway and what revision is required.

Structural finding first: the diocesan document is Common Core 9–10, essentially verbatim. Florida B.E.S.T. is a deliberate departure from Common Core, so the mismatches are not random — they cluster in the four places FLDOE consciously cut or rewrote CCSS: 1. Media / multimodal comparison — B.E.S.T. deleted the CCSS "two artistic mediums" and "accounts in different mediums" standards outright. 2. World literature — B.E.S.T. replaced the CCSS world-literature requirement with a historical American speeches-and-essays requirement (R.2.2/R.2.3). 3. Summary — B.E.S.T. replaced "objective summary" with paraphrase (R.3.2). 4. Speaking & listening — B.E.S.T. demoted all of CCSS SL to six generic K–12 "Supporting" expectations (ELA.K12.EE) with no grade-10 specificity.

A course built to Florida alone will therefore under-serve exactly those four areas. That is what the table below shows.

The one open decision — P9, collaborative discussion

This is the only gap that cannot be closed by adding a lesson. The diocesan standard requires students to work with peers to set rules for collegial discussions and decision-making, including setting clear goals, deadlines, and individual roles. The course is deliberately async and solo — that is a house convention (VR is "async, solo, with student-chosen Location; no required live VR"), and it protects the 500:1 ratio.

Three ways to close it, in ascending cost:

  1. Upgrade the two existing Discussions to require peer-negotiated ground rules and a written summary of where the thread agreed and disagreed. Cheapest. Partially satisfies 1.2 and 1.4; still no shared decision.
  2. Add one graded group deliverable per semester — M3's Players' Company is the natural fit (a theatrical company genuinely has roles, deadlines, and shared decisions). Fully satisfies the standard. Costs teacher time and breaks the solo convention for one project.
  3. Add a live seminar requirement. Fully satisfies it, but contradicts the async design and the ratio.

Option 2 is the recommendation, scoped to M3 only, because the project's own fiction already supplies the roles.

5. Note in the other direction

Florida requires several things the diocesan document does not, and these must be kept regardless: - ELA.10.R.1.3 — coming-of-age experiences and conflicting perspectives (no diocesan analogue; M2 map already flags this as uncovered in M2) - ELA.10.R.1.4 — layers of meaning/ambiguity in a poem (both M1 and M2 maps flag this as only partially covered — still an open item independent of this crosswalk) - ELA.10.R.3.3 — the classical-source window (ancient Greece/Rome, 1200 BCE–455 CE), stricter than the diocesan "source material" standard - ELA.10.R.3.4 — the named device list (irony, rhetorical question, antithesis, zeugma, metonymy, synecdoche, asyndeton), more specific than diocesan - Version requirement — approximately one-third of the 10th Grade Sample Book List used in instruction across the year

Meeting the diocesan standards is therefore additive. Nothing in the diocesan set conflicts with a Florida benchmark; the work is filling holes, not resolving contradictions.
