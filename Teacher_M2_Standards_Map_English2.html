2. Module 2 Major-benchmark coverage at a glance

Module 2 is this course's argument-and-research module, so it is the center of gravity for the writing benchmarks Module 1 could not touch (Bard's Hall was narrative). Where Module 1 carried R.1.x and C.1.2 heavily, Module 2 carries C.1.3 (argument writing), C.4.1 (research), R.3.2 (paraphrase as a named routine), plus continued R.1.1/R.1.2 and a second pass at R.3.3 (classical-text adaptation). It also picks up real, non-incidental R.3.4 (rhetoric) — Module 1's _teacher map explicitly deferred R.3.4's fuller treatment to "Q2 Oedipus, which is more argument-centered," and this module delivers on that. It still does not touch R.2.2/R.2.3/R.2.4 (the informational American-speeches benchmarks), which remain reserved for Q4.

4. Project and recitation — consolidated mapping

The Trial of Oedipus (module project, QW9–QW16 as feeders + final, 50 pts Tier 3): - ELA.10.C.1.3 — primary (the entire brief: claim, evidence, reasoning, counterclaim, response, formal/objective tone) - ELA.10.C.4.1 — the required verified JStor source and its correct citation - ELA.10.C.2.1 — the recorded 60–90 second closing argument - ELA.10.C.3.1 — conventions folded into every feeder and the final rubric (register, citation mechanics, conciseness) - ELA.10.EE.1.1 — at least three pieces of textual evidence, quoted fairly, including evidence against the student's own case - ELA.10.EE.5.1 — the formal-brief format and MLA mechanics as "accepted rules of a format" - ELA.10.R.3.3 — the project's whole premise depends on Oedipus the King as a classical text under argument

Recitation (Q2, double-duty with the anchor text, launch Wk 4 / due Wk 8, 20 pts Tier 2): - ELA.10.C.2.1 — primary (oral presentation, clear perspective, memorized/near-memorized delivery) - ELA.10.R.3.3 — the recited passage (the priest's plea or the closing chorus) is itself the classical text - ELA.10.R.1.4 — incidental: this quarter's recitation passages are genuine verse (Storr), unlike M1's prose Butler passages - ELA.10.EE.6.1 — voice/tone, supporting

5. Gaps — NOT covered in Module 2 (flagged per _BUILD-CONVENTIONS.md §14)

  • R.2.2, R.2.3, R.2.4 (historical American speeches and essays: central idea, author's purpose/appeals, comparing opposing arguments) require a Q4 American-speeches text set. Not covered anywhere in Module 2, despite this module's heavy argument focus. Do not claim these are met until the Q4 module is built; a student's skill at building an argument here does not, by itself, satisfy a benchmark that specifically requires historical American speeches as the text.
  • R.2.1 (informational text structures/features) remains reserved for a later research-heavy/informational unit. Module 2's research strand (C.4.1) teaches finding and evaluating a scholarly source, which is a different skill from analyzing informational text structures; do not conflate the two.
  • R.1.3 (coming-of-age, conflicting perspectives) is not carried by this module. Oedipus the King has no coming-of-age character arc comparable to Telemachus in M1; this benchmark needs a different vehicle in a later module.
  • C.1.2 (narrative pacing), C.1.4 (expository), C.5.1 / C.5.2 (digital presentation/collaborative platforms) are not this module's writing mode. C.1.2 was M1's mode; expect C.1.4 and C.5.1/5.2 in a later module.
  • R.3.1 (figurative language/mood) gets only light, incidental treatment here (Tiresias's paradox, the play's mounting dread); do not report this as a dedicated R.3.1 unit.
  • R.1.4 (poem ambiguity) is a partial, unclaimed opportunity, not formal coverage. This module's text (Storr) is genuine verse and the recitation passages are literally poetry, a real improvement over M1's prose-Butler situation, but no lesson builds a dedicated ambiguity-in-a-poem analysis. Flag for a future build pass rather than claim coverage now.

Woven into existing required lessons (not separately publishable)

  • M2_W3_L3.03_Say_It_Faithfully_Then_Weigh_It.html, new Part 3. Distinguishes paraphrase (one passage, same length, nothing omitted) from summary (whole text, far shorter, most detail omitted on purpose), then teaches the asymmetry that makes summary an advocate's problem: a paraphrase betrays a text by adding, a summary by leaving out. New objective 3, plus an ow-select whose four options are all factually accurate and only one of which is a faithful summary. Closes diocesan Reading Literature Key Ideas 2 and Reading Informational Text Key Ideas 2 ("providing an accurate summary of the text based upon this analysis"). Florida replaced summary with paraphrase (R.3.2), so this had no FL host. Before this change, "summarize" appeared in the course only inside AI callouts, never as student instruction.
  • M2_W7_L7.01_The_Quote_That_Earns_Its_Place.html, new Part 3. A triage table for quotation vs. paraphrase vs. summary: length, when to reach for each, whether it needs quotation marks, whether it needs a citation. The teaching point is the last two columns read across: the quotation-mark column changes by row, the citation column never does. Also corrected a wording error in Part 1, where Version B was labeled "an accurate summary" when it is in fact a paraphrase. No new widget (the lesson already carries its §5 allowance); the practice is a notebook prompt pointing at L7.02.
  • M2_W7_L7.02_Citing_Like_a_Professional.html, new Parts 5 and 6. Part 5 teaches the three ways credit goes missing: the unmarked copy (exact words with no quotation marks, even when cited), the uncredited paraphrase (the most common and most surprising), and the half-changed sentence (synonyms swapped, structure kept). Plus a three-question fix and an ow-select in which the clean option and one plagiarizing option are the same sentence, differing only by the citation. Part 6 is the Source Work Sheet, a downloadable tool. New objectives 4 and 5. Closes diocesan Writing, Research 2 ("avoiding plagiarism and following a standard format for citation"). Florida requires neither by name; EE.1.1's 9-12 note asks only that students be aware that style guides differ. Before this change, "plagiar" appeared nowhere in the course's 161 files.
  • M2_W5_ProjectPitch_Discussion.md revised. Step 0 amends the Hall Rules the class set in M1 W7, which closes the decision-making loop rather than only opening it; the two replies split into Devil's Advocate and Synthesizer roles. Workload unchanged. Closes diocesan Speaking and Listening 1.2 (ground rules, individual roles) and 1.4 (summarizing agreement and disagreement, qualifying one's own view). The Synthesizer reply requires naming a genuinely strong argument against oneself, which is the same move as QW12 and gives a diagnostic read on it a week early.
