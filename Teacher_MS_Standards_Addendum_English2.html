The MS Designation — What It Means and How to Use It

English 2 (1001340) and English 2 Honors (1001350) | Kings and Community Created 2026-08-01 from _DIOCESAN-CROSSWALK-English2.md

The short version, for a teacher in a hurry

Some items in this course are marked MS. They exist to meet Mississippi diocesan standards (Diocese of Jackson, Office of Education, English Language Arts Standards, English II) that have no Florida CPALMS equivalent.

  • If your school answers to Florida only: you may leave every MS item unpublished. The course is fully Florida-compliant without them. They are genuine enrichment, not filler, but nothing else depends on them.
  • If your school answers to the diocesan standards: publish them. Without them, specific diocesan standards are not met anywhere in the course.
  • Nothing is broken either way. No MS item is a prerequisite for a graded item, appears in another lesson's "What You'll Do Next" as a requirement, or affects the gradebook weights. Publish or unpublish freely, and change your mind mid-year if you need to.

How to spot an MS item

The designation is deliberately quiet, so students are not reading standards codes off their own lesson pages (build conventions §13 forbids that). It shows up in exactly two places:

  1. The lesson or item number. Lesson 4.MS1, Lesson 4.MS2, L6.MS1, L2.MS1. If there is an MS in the number, it is an MS item.
  2. The filename. M2_W4_L4.MS1_When_an_Argument_Breaks.html, M2_W9_Council_Deliberation_MS_Discussion.md.

A student sees a lesson number. A teacher scanning the Canvas module list sees the same number and knows, from this document, what it signifies. There is no banner, no badge, and no standards code anywhere on the student page.

Why these gaps exist at all

The diocesan standards are Common Core 9-10, essentially verbatim. Florida B.E.S.T. was written as a deliberate departure from Common Core. The mismatches are therefore not random: they cluster in the four places FLDOE consciously cut or rewrote Common Core.

  1. Media and multimodal comparison. B.E.S.T. deleted both Common Core "different mediums" standards outright.
  2. World literature. B.E.S.T. replaced the Common Core world-literature requirement with a historical American speeches-and-essays requirement.
  3. Summary. B.E.S.T. replaced "objective summary" with paraphrase.
  4. Speaking and listening. B.E.S.T. demoted all of Common Core's speaking-and-listening strand to six generic K-12 "Supporting" expectations with no grade-10 specificity.

A course built to Florida alone will under-serve exactly those four areas. Everything below is a response to one of them.

Publish-decision guide

If you publish nothing MS: Florida-complete. Diocesan gaps remaining: fallacy identification, speaker evaluation, cross-medium comparison, and the decision-making clause of collaborative discussion. Summary, paraphrase, plagiarism, discussion roles, and agreement-summarizing are still covered, because those were woven into required lessons.

If you publish the three built S1 items (L4.MS1, L4.MS2, M2_W9): every diocesan Speaking-and-Listening and argument-evaluation standard is met for Semester 1. Cost: two ungraded lessons in Week 4, and one Tier 2 discussion in Week 9 at 10 points. Week 4 is a light week (no recitation event, no new reading after Week 5), so it absorbs two lessons without strain.

Recommended if you are diocesan: publish all three. Publish L4.MS1 and L4.MS2 together and in order, since MS2 depends on MS1's six named breaks and its "a label is only half an objection" rule. Publishing MS2 alone would leave its listening-sheet check referring to a lesson students never saw.

If you publish MS2 without MS1, edit the two places MS2 names Lesson 4.MS1 by number: the Today's Focus paragraph, and the listening-sheet check message about the label-as-objection trap. (The reminder line in MS2's downloaded file states the same rule but does not name the lesson, so it needs no change.)

Points and gradebook

Only one MS item is graded: M2_W9_Council_Deliberation_MS_Discussion.md, at 10 points, Tier 2, in the Reading & Participation category (20% for Standard, 15% for Honors).

Because Canvas assignment groups are weighted (§12a, locked), adding or removing a 10-point item inside a weighted category does not change anyone's grade scale. The category still counts for the same percentage; the points inside it simply redistribute. So publishing or unpublishing this discussion is safe at any point in the term, including mid-quarter.

The two MS lessons are ungraded content pages and touch nothing.

For the Honors course

MS items are part of the shared Standard base, so Honors inherits them by the normal build-once-assemble-twice rule (§0). No Honors-only MS items exist. If you publish an MS item in Standard, publish it in Honors too, or the two courses drift.

The Honors depth delta applies as usual where relevant: an Honors section running M2_W9 should expect the Holdout role to be genuinely argued rather than performed, and can reasonably require the Aristotle Poetics excerpt to inform the deliberation.
